How to pay for home care

November 2026 by Dedicated Care Living

Most families pay for home care with a mix of personal savings, long-term care insurance and other programs. Medicare generally does not cover ongoing non-medical home care, so it helps to plan early and understand your options.

The common ways families pay for home care

Medicare generally does not cover ongoing custodial or non-medical home care, such as companionship, bathing assistance, or routine help with daily living. Some long-term care insurance policies may provide benefits for eligible home care services, depending on the policy. Dedicated Care Living can help families understand the documentation associated with their home care services, although coverage decisions are made by the insurance provider.

Long-term care insurance

We work with families who have long-term care insurance and can provide appropriate service documentation to support their claims. Coverage, reimbursement requirements, benefit limits, and payment arrangements depend on the individual’s insurance policy. We encourage families to verify their benefits directly with their insurer.

Veterans and other programs

Some veterans and surviving spouses may qualify for benefits through the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs that help with the cost of care. Other programs may be available through state and local agencies, depending on income and eligibility. Rules and amounts change, so we encourage families to check directly with the VA or the local Area Agency on Aging, and to ask a benefits specialist about their own situation.

Planning with an advisor

Because the right way to pay depends on savings, insurance, income and long-term goals, many families find it helpful to speak with a financial planner, elder law attorney or benefits specialist. We are not financial advisors, but we are happy to explain our services, provide documentation and help you think through the care schedule that fits your plan.
