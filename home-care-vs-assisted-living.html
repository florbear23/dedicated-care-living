Home care vs assisted living

November 2026 by Dedicated Care Living

Home care brings support to your loved one's own house, while assisted living means moving to a community. Neither is right for everyone. The best choice depends on health, safety, budget and what matters most to your family.

What home care and assisted living each provide

Home care means a caregiver comes to your loved one's house to help with daily life, such as bathing, dressing, meals, medication reminders, housekeeping and company. Assisted living is a residential community where staff provide meals, activities and help with daily tasks in a shared setting. Both aim to keep older adults safe and supported, but they do it in very different environments.

How they compare on cost, independence and family time

Home care lets your loved one stay in a familiar place, keep their own routine and see neighbors and family as usual. Care can be a few hours a week or around the clock, and you pay only for the hours you use. Assisted living offers a built-in community and a predictable monthly fee, but it means moving and adjusting to new surroundings. Families often find that one-to-one attention at home feels more personal than shared staff in a community.

When home care is the better fit

Home care is often a good fit when your loved one wants to stay in their own home, has a safe space to live, and needs help rather than constant supervision. It can also work well after a hospital stay, for couples who want to stay together, or for someone with memory loss who benefits from familiar surroundings. You can browse the full range of options on our services page.

How to decide as a family

Start with an honest look at what your loved one needs each day and what they want. Ask: Are they safe at home, including at night? Which tasks are becoming difficult? What does your loved one say they want? How much can the family help? Visit communities if you are considering them, and talk with a care team about what home care could look like. You do not have to decide everything at once, and many families begin with a few hours of help and adjust.
